## Bottom line

- **R4 (SFT + fact sheet) scores 0.5840** on the 498-item denominator.
  SFT alone (R3) scores 0.5220. The base model with the same fact sheet
  (R2) scores 0.2060, and the untrained baseline (R0) scores 0.1726.
- **Fine-tuning is the large effect.** R4 and R2 see identical user
  messages, so the fact sheet is held fixed. R4 beats R2 by **+0.379**

  exact prompts of Phase 12 pass 1. Pass 1 completed 425 answers. Of

## 5. Paired comparisons

Each comparison uses the items scored in both arms. Statistics:

- **Δ** is the mean of the per-item differences.
- **Wins / losses / ties** count items where A scores above, below or
  equal to B.
- **Sign test.** Two-sided exact binomial test on wins against losses,
  with ties dropped.
- **95% CI.** Percentile bootstrap of the mean difference: 10,000
  resamples of items, `numpy.random.default_rng(0)`, fresh for each
  comparison.

| A vs B | n | mean A | mean B | Δ (A−B) | 95% CI | wins / losses / ties | sign test p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| R4_sft_rag vs R2_base_rag | 497 | 0.5852 | 0.2060 | **+0.3792** | [+0.3236, +0.4337] | 332 / 76 / 89 | 3.07e-39 |
| R4_sft_rag vs R2_base_rag_unwrapped | 487 | 0.5791 | 0.2912 | **+0.2879** | [+0.2306, +0.3446] | 307 / 104 / 76 | 2.38e-24 |
| R4_sft_rag vs R3_sft | 487 | 0.5807 | 0.5220 | **+0.0586** | [+0.0229, +0.0939] | 105 / 59 / 323 | 4.08e-04 |
| R2_base_rag_unwrapped vs R0_baseline | 487 | 0.2912 | 0.1730 | **+0.1183** | [+0.0710, +0.1667] | 172 / 134 / 181 | 0.034 |

What each comparison does and does not isolate:

- **R4 vs R2: fine-tuning, fact sheet held fixed.**
  - The user messages are byte-identical.
  - The system prompts differ. R2 carries the output contract so the
    base model knows the schema; R4 learned the schema in training.
  - So the contrast is "SFT against schema prompting", with retrieval
    identical. It is the cleanest comparison here.
- **R4 vs R2_unwrapped: the same, with one formatting slip forgiven.**
  - Unwrapping removes about 0.09 of the gap (+0.379 → +0.288). Most of
    the gap is substance, not packaging.
- **R4 vs R3: the fact sheet on top of SFT.**
  - This comparison also changes the cap (1,200 → 3,072; R3 lost 2
    answers to it) and the environment (R3's was not recorded).
  - Ties dominate: 323 of 487 items.
  - The effect is clearly non-zero: the CI excludes 0, and the lower end
    (+0.023) is still eight times the noise floor in §7.
- **R2_unwrapped vs R0: three changes at once on the base model.**
  - The base model gains the output contract, the fact sheet and the
    longer cap together.
  - The effect is positive but the weakest of the four: p = 0.034, with
    172 items up and 134 down.
  - R1 (base + contract, no fact sheet) was not run, so the three causes
    cannot be separated.

## 6. R2's `{"Advisory": …}` wrapping and G2 failures (Task C)

- **Wrapping.** 132 of R2's 500 responses (all inside the 498) are
  exactly `{"Advisory": {...}}`. R4 has 0. The base model apparently
  reads "matching the Advisory schema" as "nest under an `Advisory`
  key".
- **How the flag works.** `--unwrap-advisory` scores the inner object.
  It acts only on an exact single key `Advisory` whose value is an
  object. It never edits the responses file, and it marks each unwrapped
  item in the score file.

R2's five most common G2 failure reasons, unwrapping off. R2 has 166 G2
failures in the 498. The counts are items, and an item can carry more
than one reason.

| # | reason | items |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | wrapped in `{"Advisory": …}`, so the top level is missing `in_scope`, `query_understood` and `escalate_to_expert` | 132 |
| 2 | `unknown PHI requires escalate_to_expert=True`: recommends a row with `phi_days` null and does not escalate | 17 |
| 3 | `chemical_options.*.dose.raw` missing | 6 |
| 4 | `chemical_options.*.dose.unit` outside `g / ml / kg / l / %` | 5 |
| 5 | `likely_causes.*.type` outside the five allowed types | 5 |

- **After unwrapping**, 99 R2 answers still fail G2 (65 of them were
  wrapped). They are led by the unknown-PHI invariant (67). Next come a
  missing `dose.raw` (11) and fractional `spray_volume_*_l_per_acre`
  values (10 and 9).
- **R4's 45 G2 failures are all the unknown-PHI invariant.**
  - This is the main reason R4's G2 rate (91.0%) sits below R3's

    type outside the allowed set). Its other 2 G2 misses are the
    cut-offs.

## 7. Noise floor (Task D)

R4 and Phase 12 pass 1 (`rag_B_responses.jsonl`) share the system prompt,
the adapter, and byte-identical user messages (`rag_prompts_B.jsonl`).

**What differs between the two runs:**
- max_new_tokens: 1,200 → 3,072.
- max_seq_length: 4,096 → 5,911.
- The library install. Pass 1 recorded no versions.

**What may differ: decoding.** Pass 1 did not record its decoding
settings. Even so, 320 answers come back byte-identical (median 210
tokens, up to 1,187), which would be very unlikely under a different
`repetition_penalty`. So pass 1 very likely ran at Qwen's default of
1.05, the same as R4.

- **"Hit its limit"** means one of pass 1's 75 cut-offs, the items Phase
  12 regenerated. The script asserts that this set equals pass 1's

  1's text exactly.

- **Text noise is common, but score noise is not.**
  - A quarter of completed answers (105/425) change in text when the

  - Only 4 of them change score, all four upward. The divergence sits
    almost entirely in prose (evidence, caution, non-chemical advice)

  - **On a finished answer, run-to-run noise on this metric is about

  - It is one repeat across one environment change, not a distribution
    over many. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude bound, not a standard
    error.

    74 that are scored, and 42 still score 0 on their completed answers.
- **The scored-in-both line mixes these two effects.** Its +0.0653 is
  almost all the cap. It is not noise.

## 9. Limitations

1. **The base prompt omits one schema rule.** The output contract in
   `system_prompt_base.txt` lists four of the schema's five invariants.
   It leaves out "a dose basis of `unstated` requires
   `escalate_to_expert=true`".
   - Only R2 sees the contract, so a base-model answer could fail G2 on
     a rule it was never shown.
   - In this run that happened **0 times**: no R2 failure, wrapped or
     not, involves the `unstated` rule.
   - The rule is left as it is. The contract is generate_sft's
     `OUTPUT_CONTRACT_TEXT` verbatim, and changing it would change R2's
     prompt.
2. **`resolve_key` shares steps with the gold tagger.**
   - Arm B's retrieval key equals the bench's gold key on **80.7% of
     KCC-sourced items (306/379)**, against **65.3% of constructed and
     label_db items (79/121)** (Phase 12, Confound 1).
   - The fact sheet is also drawn from the table `verify.py` grades
     against.
   - Both R2 and R4 use these fact sheets, so the bias cancels in R4 vs
     R2. It does not cancel in R4 vs R3 or R2 vs R0.
3. **Library drift between runs.**
   - R2 and R4 differ in torch build, transformers, peft and the Python
     patch release (§8).
   - R0, R3 and pass 1 recorded nothing.
   - §7 measures the effect of one such change on scores (about 0.003),
     not of every possible one.
4. **`application_method` is None in scoring.**
   - `score.py` keeps the Phase 10 convention: `VerifyContext` gets no
     application method.
   - So candidate label rows are not narrowed by method (foliar, drench,
     seed treatment) when an answer is graded.
   - This applies equally to every arm.
5. **R1 (base model + output contract, no fact sheet) was not run.**
   - R2_unwrapped − R0 (+0.118) mixes the contract, the fact sheet and
     the longer cap.
   - The base model's gain from the fact sheet alone is therefore
     unknown.
6. **Different caps.**
   - R0 and R3 ran at 1,200 tokens; R3 lost 2 answers to it.
   - R2 and R4 ran at 3,072 and lost none.
   - R4 − R3 includes that change, but the cap can account for at most
     2 of the 487 paired items: R3's two cut-offs (`S1_424`, `B3_17`),
     which score 0 by construction.
7. **`R2_base_rag_unwrapped` is a scoring sensitivity, not model
   output.** Report R2_base_rag as the base arm's score, and the
   unwrapped figure only alongside it.
8. **A ground-truth error in label_db: seed-treatment doses recorded per
   hectare.** See §10. It does not move any headline figure here.

## 10. Ground-truth error: seed-treatment basis

### The error

**Where it comes from.** CIB&RC heads its dose column "Dosage/ha
(Formulation)" (fungicides p.43). For seed-treatment products, though,
that column usually holds a dose per kg of **seed**, or per 10 kg or
100 kg.

**When the parser gets it right.** If the cell says so ("8-10 ml/kg
seed"), label_db records `per_kg_seed`.

**When it doesn't.** If the cell is a bare number, the parser took the
header literally and recorded `per_ha`. `verify()` then derives a
per-acre figure and accepts per-acre answers against it.

For example, `S1_140` scores 1.000 while recommending 1.6187 ml per acre
of two Sedaxane + Fludioxonil + Thiamethoxam FS products. Their cotton
label dose is 4.0 ml per kg of seed.

### What it touches

An option counts as using a row if `verify()` would grade it against
that row. That is `for_triple` on crop, pest and a.i., then `verify()`'s
own candidate narrowing.

- **Benchmark: 7 items** have a gold advisory that uses a WRONG_BASIS
  row.
  - `B3_25`, `B4b_4`, `S1_1261` use row #152.
  - `S1_101`, `S1_140`, `S1_180`, `S1_5582` use rows #629/#630.
  - 5 more use UNCLEAR rows; those are not excluded.
- **Model answers graded against a WRONG_BASIS row:** R0 2, R3 2, R2 10,
  R4 5 (R4 guarded 5). Counting gold and all arms together: 18 items.
- **SFT data:** **52 of 4,493** training examples and **11 of 1,136** test
  examples use a WRONG_BASIS row. Another 14 and 2 use UNCLEAR rows.
